An agent reading your instructions is as intelligent as you. It infers, reasons, and decides. Write to that.

Keep instructions simple and short; add detail only when it changes the agent's behavior.

Hand over what it cannot infer: the outcome, why it matters, the constraints, what finished looks like. The route is its own. A step earns its place where the default would go wrong or order carries meaning. Written anyway, it competes with the reader's judgment, and the step wins.

## Pointers

A pointer sends the reader elsewhere and names the cases that should follow it. Its wording, not the material, decides whether the reader arrives. Front-load the term it knows, one trigger per case, nothing the target already says.

## Disclosure

Inline what every run needs. Push out what only some reach. Keep a concept's parts together.

Length past need thins attention. Split when one case earns its own file, not when the file grows.

## Pruning

One meaning, one place. Restating what the workspace already says (AGENTS.md, coding standards, docs) buys a copy that goes stale. Keep the convention, the reason, the trap.

Delete whole sentences that leave the reader unchanged. The default is the reader's, so test instead of debate. A word too weak to move it needs a stronger one, not a longer one.

## Say what to do

Name the target behaviour. A prohibition plants what it bans. Pair any hard guardrail with the target.
